## New entries added
- **MedSpa OS** (73): Vertical practice management platform purpose-built for independent medical spas. Incumbents (Zenoti, PatientNow, Vagaro) rated below 4.0 by MedSpa owners for missing aesthetic-specific workflows. 22% YoY market growth in Q1 2026 and 31 Capterra complaints specifically about "not designed for aesthetics" create a validated, reachable target market.
- **SLADesk** (73): Internal SLA tracking for the 20-150 employee "Goldilocks" market — too small for ServiceNow, too multi-department for Jira Service Management. Hybrid work eliminated informal accountability; no purpose-built tool exists for cross-department SLA visibility. Highly solo-buildable (17/20) with zero direct competitors in this segment.
- **SaaSScope** (72): SaaS spend rationalization for 20-100 person companies. Average SMB wastes 30% of software budget on zombie and duplicate subscriptions. Enterprise tools (Torii, Zylo) start at $500+/month; the SMB tier below $200/month is unoccupied by a capable self-serve tool.

## Biggest movers
**MatchFlow** is the week's only improver (+3), jumping from #9 to #3. Botkeeper — the incumbent AI bookkeeping platform for accounting firms — shut down on February 7, 2026, leaving hundreds of firms without automation and actively searching for alternatives. This is the most powerful market validation event in this week's scan: the problem is real, the market is paying, and the leading product just exited. Booke AI is the main new direct competitor capturing Botkeeper refugees, but the B2B2B model (one bookkeeper subscription covering 5-20 end accounts) and direct targeting of displaced Botkeeper customers gives MatchFlow a specific, time-limited acquisition advantage.

**ComplianceLayer** is the week's biggest decliner (-7), driven entirely by the EU AI Act Digital Omnibus adoption. The 16-month extension of high-risk system deadlines (August 2026 → December 2027) substantially reduces the immediate urgency that made "ongoing compliance management" feel pressing for AI startups in June. The product's core thesis — continuous compliance vs. one-time documentation — survives the delay, but the acute demand signal weakens considerably.

## Market signals worth watching

1. **Spec-driven development and spec-to-code drift**: AWS Kiro, GitHub Spec Kit, and Thoughtworks' Technology Radar all validated spec-driven development as a mainstream discipline in 2026. SpecSeal (OSS, 0 stars as of May 2026) launched to solve the "specs and code drift apart after shipping" problem with no commercial product existing. A CI gate for spec coverage (like test coverage but for requirements) scored 71/100 this week — just below the top 10 threshold. If the spec-driven dev market grows as fast as it has been, this could enter the cookbook within 2-4 weeks.

2. **AI agent human-in-the-loop approval workflows**: The "agentic workflow collapse" research from June 2026 confirmed governance is the dominant failure mode for production AI agent deployments. The specific gap: when an AI agent needs to take a high-risk action (send an email, modify a database record, make a purchase), there is no standard tool for routing that action to a human approver via Slack before execution. ApprovalFlow scored 71/100 — this is a cleaner, narrower problem than the full governance market that Prufer and Kakunin are addressing, and may be more solo-buildable.

3. **Botkeeper refugee market consolidation**: Botkeeper's February 2026 shutdown is still rippling through the accounting firm community. The 6-month window where displaced firms are actively evaluating alternatives is approximately halfway through. Any builder in the bookkeeping automation space should move immediately — this acquisition channel degrades every week as firms settle into new tools (primarily Booke AI, Docyt, and Integra Balance AI).
